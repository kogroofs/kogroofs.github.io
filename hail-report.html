DFW hail report: where hail hit, month by month

Updated every morning from NOAA weather radar. Last checked October 27, 2026.

The short answer

So far this October, NOAA weather radar hasn't picked up hail 1 inch or larger in any of the towns we serve. The last day it did was September 27, 2026, with about 1.5 inches near Weatherford. In 2026 so far, there have been 40 days with 1-inch hail in at least one town we serve. The biggest was about 4 inches near Cedar Hill on June 6, 2026.

Wondering about your own street? Check your address against the same radar records. Hail damage often doesn't leak right away, so a roof that looks fine from the ground can still be bruised.

How this report works

Every morning, our system downloads hail detections from NOAA's NEXRAD weather radars for the Dallas-Fort Worth area. A town counts as hit when radar estimated hail 1 inch or larger within 3 miles of the center of one of its ZIP codes. One inch is about the size of a quarter. That's where the National Weather Service calls hail severe, and where shingle damage gets common.

These are radar estimates of hail size, not measurements on the ground. Radar can miss small or short bursts, stone sizes vary block to block, and a town on this list doesn't mean every roof in it was damaged. It tells you where to look.

Reporters, neighborhood groups, and other websites are welcome to use this report. Please link back to this page.

Where this comes from

  • NOAA National Centers for Environmental Information: Severe Weather Data Inventory (radar hail signatures)
  • NOAA Storm Prediction Center: storm reports
