Will insurance pay for a new roof after hail?

Updated October 2026 · For Texas homeowners

The short answer

Often, but not always. Hail is usually a covered peril on a Texas homeowners policy. Whether you get a new roof, and how much of it insurance pays, comes down to four things: what the hail actually did to your roof, your deductible, how your policy pays for roofs, and any exclusions. Your policy and your insurance company make the call. This page shows you how it works, so you know what to ask.

The four things that decide a hail roof claim

1. What the hail actually did to your roof

Insurance pays for damage the storm caused, not for a roof that's simply old. On shingles, that means hits that cracked or bruised the shingle or knocked the granules off down to the mat, usually heaviest on the slopes that faced the storm. Normal granule loss, blistering from a hot attic, and worn-out shingles are wear, and wear isn't covered. Here's how to tell hail damage from wear.

In Texas, it's on the homeowner to show what caused the damage. That's why the date of the storm, dated photos, and a written report that goes slope by slope matter so much.

2. Your deductible

Many Texas policies now have a separate wind and hail deductible that's a percentage of your home's insured value. A 2% deductible on a home insured for $300,000 is $6,000. If the damage costs less than your deductible to fix, the claim pays nothing, and even a claim that pays nothing can show up in your claim history. Find your deductible on your declarations page before you file.

3. How your policy pays for a roof

  • Replacement cost (RCV). Pays what it costs to replace the roof, minus your deductible. Most insurers pay it in two parts: first the roof's depreciated value, then the rest, called recoverable depreciation or holdback, after the work is done and you send proof. Many policies set a deadline to finish the work and collect it, so find yours.
  • Actual cash value (ACV). Pays what the roof was worth before the storm, after depreciation for its age, minus your deductible. On an older roof, that can be far less than a new roof costs.
  • Roof payment schedules. Some Texas policies pay a set percentage of the roof's cost based on its age. The older the roof, the smaller the percentage.

4. Exclusions and endorsements

A cosmetic damage exclusion leaves out hail dents that change how a roof looks without hurting how it works. It's common on metal and impact-resistant roofs. Some policies add other limits on roofs. Search your policy for the words "cosmetic" and "roof," and ask your agent what any endorsement means.

How a hail roof claim works in Texas

  1. You report the damage

    Call your insurance company or agent and write down your claim number. Don't wait. Many policies set a deadline for reporting damage, often one year from the storm. Here's every deadline on a hail claim.

  2. The insurer acknowledges your claim

    Texas law gives your insurance company 15 business days to tell you it received your claim. It may ask you for more information.

  3. An adjuster inspects the roof

    You can have your roofer there to point out the damage they found.

  4. The insurer decides

    It has 15 business days after it gets what it needs from you to accept or deny the claim, and it can take up to 45 more days if it tells you why. A denial has to be explained in writing.

  5. You get paid

    Once your insurer agrees to pay, Texas law gives it 5 business days to send the payment. If you have a mortgage, the check is often made out to you and your mortgage company together.

  6. The roof goes on, and you collect the rest

    On a replacement cost policy, send the final invoice to your insurer to collect the holdback.

Claim denied or paid too little? What to do next

A denial isn't always the last word. Take it in this order.

  1. Get the reason in writing. Your insurance company has to tell you in writing why it denied all or part of your claim. Common reasons are wear and tear, cosmetic damage only, damage from an older storm, or a total that's under your deductible.
  2. Compare it to what's on your roof. Lay the denial next to your inspection report and photos. If the report shows storm damage the adjuster missed, that's your case.
  3. Tell the company why you disagree. Send your roofer's report, photos, and estimate, and ask them to take another look. Insurance companies do overlook things.
  4. Use appraisal, if your policy has it. You and the insurer each hire an appraiser, and the two appraisers pick an umpire. Appraisal settles how much damage there is and what it costs to fix, not whether it's covered. You pay your appraiser and half the umpire. Check your policy for its deadline.
  5. File a complaint with the Texas Department of Insurance. Call the TDI help line at 800-252-3439, Monday through Friday, 8 AM to 5 PM, or file online.
  6. Get help if you need it. A licensed public adjuster can negotiate the claim for you. In Texas, their fee can't be more than 10% of the claim. An attorney is the other route.

And a straight word: sometimes a denial is right. If your roof is worn out rather than storm-damaged, we'll tell you so, and give you an honest price on a repair or on a new roof you pay for yourself.

Questions to ask your agent before you file

  • What's my wind and hail deductible, in dollars?
  • Does my policy pay replacement cost or actual cash value on the roof? Is there a roof payment schedule?
  • Do I have a cosmetic damage exclusion?
  • What's my deadline to report damage, and to finish repairs and collect any holdback?
  • Does my policy pay for code upgrades if the city requires them?
