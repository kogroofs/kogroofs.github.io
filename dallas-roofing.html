Where we work

Roofing in Dallas and Dallas County

Dallas, Irving, Grand Prairie, Garland, Mesquite, Richardson, and north into Collin County

The short answer

KOG Roofing works on commercial buildings and homes throughout Dallas and the cities around it. We fix leaks, replace roofs, and inspect after storms on flat, metal, and shingle roofs. Every job comes with photos and a written price before work starts.

Storms that hit Dallas

  • June 13, 2012. Hail up to 4.25 inches fell in parts of Dallas and Collin counties, according to the National Weather Service. Early damage estimates ran about $900 million.
  • October 20, 2019. An EF-3 tornado cut across North Dallas at night, damaging homes, schools, and businesses along its path.

Big storms make the news, but the ordinary spring hailstorm is what wears out most Dallas roofs. If one came through your part of town, our storm damage inspections give you a written report with photos. For what to do first, read After a hail or wind storm.

Landmark districts

Dallas has historic neighborhoods, such as Swiss Avenue and Munger Place, where homes sit in city landmark districts. There, a Certificate of Appropriateness is required before exterior work. The city treats shingle replacement with no change in color as routine maintenance that staff can approve. Changing the color or making other changes to the roof can mean a review by the Landmark Commission, which takes longer. If you're in a landmark district, start that process early. We'll give you the product, color, and spec sheet for your application.

Permits in Dallas

Permit and code rules depend on your address, the building, and what the job involves, and a roof replacement can bring in energy code requirements. We check what the City of Dallas, or your suburb, requires for your address and scope before work starts, and we get any permit the job needs.

Commercial roofing in Dallas

Dallas has every kind of commercial roof there is, and each comes with its own problems:

  • Older brick buildings in places like Deep Ellum and the Design District, often with flat roofs, parapet walls, and several layers of old roofing under the top one.
  • Office buildings and medical offices with rooftop units that other trades walk on and cut into.
  • Retail strips and restaurants where a leak over the sales floor or the kitchen can shut a tenant down.
  • Warehouses and shops with large TPO or metal roofs and a lot of drains and seams to keep up with.

For property managers, we put findings in a written report with photos that you can take to an owner. See commercial roofing.
