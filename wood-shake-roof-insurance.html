Insurance dropping you over a cedar or wood shake roof?

Updated November 2026 · For Texas homeowners

The short answer

Find your deadline first. Then ask your insurance company, in writing, exactly what's wrong and what kind of roof it will insure. Your choices are metal, shingles, synthetic shakes that look like cedar, or new wood. If you love the look of wood, ask your company what it will insure before you pick one.

Why insurance companies back away from wood roofs

  • Fire. Wood burns, and a dry wood roof can catch from wind-blown embers. Untreated wood shakes have no fire rating at all. Shakes treated with a fire retardant can earn a Class B rating, or Class A when they go on over the right underlayment.
  • Upkeep. Wood needs more upkeep than shingles or metal, and it splits and rots as it ages. A company can also flag a wood roof for its condition, the same as any other roof.

Texas's 2026 rule says a company can't turn you down or drop you just because your roof is old. That rule is about age. Roof type is a separate question: the state's own reporting instructions tell insurance companies how to code it when they won't cover certain kinds of roofs. That also means Texas's new ZIP code data can't tell you how many wood roofs are getting letters, because those refusals get filed under "other."

Not sure your letter is about the wood at all? If it's about the roof's age or condition, read Can my insurance drop me because my roof is old?

Your deadline

If you bought or renewed your policy in 2024 or later, your company has to give you at least 60 days' notice before it doesn't renew. For policies turned down, canceled, or not renewed since January 1, 2026, the company has to tell you in writing why.

Sixty days goes fast. You need time to get prices, choose a roof, get it on the schedule, and get the paperwork to your agent. Don't let your coverage lapse. If you have a mortgage and your insurance ends, your lender can put a policy on the house and bill you for it, and that policy doesn't cover your belongings.

Ask your insurance company these questions, in writing

  1. Is the problem the wood itself, or the roof's condition?

    If it's condition, a repair might be enough. If it's the wood, a repair won't fix it.

  2. What roof types will you insure?

    Metal, shingles, Class 4 shingles, fire-retardant-treated wood? Get the answer before you sign a roofing contract.

  3. If I replace the roof before my deadline, will you renew?

    Ask what proof they want: the invoice, photos, or the state's impact-resistant roof form.

  4. What if the new roof is signed but not finished by the deadline?

    Ask whether a signed contract and a start date buy you any time. Get the answer in writing.

  5. Will the new roof earn a premium credit?

    Roofing rated Class 4 for impact gets the highest credit in Texas. Each company sets its own amount, and some pair the credit with an exclusion for cosmetic hail damage. With that exclusion, hail dents that don't cause leaks aren't covered. Ask about both.

Your four options

Metal

Metal doesn't burn, it can last 40 years or more when it's installed right, and many metal panels are rated Class 4 for impact. We tear off the wood, put down a new plywood deck, and install standing seam or exposed-fastener panels. At our posted prices, metal panels run $645 to $860 a square, and standing seam runs $995 to $1,325 a square. A square is 100 square feet of roof. See what it comes to for your home with the metal roof cost calculator, and read is a metal roof worth it?

Shingles

Architectural shingles cost the least up front: $500 to $750 a square at our posted prices. We tear off the wood and put down a new plywood deck, because shingles have to go on a solid deck. Class 4 impact-resistant shingles cost more, but they can earn the Texas premium credit. Read what's the best shingle for Texas heat and hail?

Synthetic shakes

Love the cedar look, but your insurance company won't take wood? Synthetic shakes are made from a composite material molded to look like real cedar shakes. Many are rated Class 4 for impact and Class A for fire, like DaVinci's composite shake. Not every product is: Brava's cedar shake comes in both Class A and Class C fire ratings, so ask for the Class A version. Ask your insurance company before you pick one, the same as any roof. We install synthetic shake roofs, tear off the old wood first, and price them after we inspect.

New wood

Wood roofs are beautiful, and we install new cedar shakes. Get your insurance company's answer in writing first, so a new wood roof doesn't bring you the same letter. Ask whether fire-retardant-treated shakes make a difference to them. We price new wood roofs after we inspect.

Why it starts with a new deck

Wood shakes are often nailed to boards with gaps between them, called spaced sheathing. That works for wood, but the building code requires a solid deck under shingles. The code does allow some metal roofs to go over old wood shakes with extra steps. We don't do it that way. We tear off the wood so we can see the framing, fix anything rotten, and put the new roof on solid plywood, and so there's no old wood left under your new roof.

A typical 32-square roof takes about 100 sheets of 4x8 plywood. We measure your roof and put the deck in your written price.

If no company will insure your home

Shop around before you give up. Another company may look at your roof differently, and an independent agent can check several companies at once. If at least two companies turn you down, you can buy a policy through the Texas FAIR Plan, the state's insurer of last resort, through a licensed agent. FAIR Plan policies cover fewer things than a regular homeowners policy, and they generally pay actual cash value unless you add replacement cost coverage where it's offered, so read the policy closely.

Common questions

Can my insurance company drop me for having a wood roof in Texas?

Texas's 2026 rule stops companies from turning you down or dropping you just because your roof is old. That rule is about age, not roof type, and the state's reporting rules have a code for companies that won't cover certain kinds of roofs. Your company has to tell you its reason in writing. If you think it's unfair, call the Texas Department of Insurance at 800-252-3439.

Will my insurance pay to replace my wood roof?

Not just because it's old or worn out. Insurance pays for covered damage, like damage from hail or wind. If a storm damaged your roof, talk to your agent about a claim.

Can you put a metal roof over my wood shakes?

The building code allows some metal roofs to go over old wood shakes with extra steps. We don't do it. We tear off the wood and put the metal on a new plywood deck, so we can fix anything rotten underneath and there's no old wood left under your roof.

Will a new roof lower my insurance premium?

It can. Texas insurers can give a premium credit for roofing rated for impact, and Class 4 gets the most. Each company sets its own amount, and some pair the credit with an exclusion for cosmetic hail damage, so ask your agent about both before you choose.
