Roof guides

Your insurance company says your roof needs work. Now what?

Updated November 2026 · For Dallas-Fort Worth homeowners

The short answer

Don't panic, and don't sign for a new roof yet. Find out exactly what your insurance company found, get the roof checked by someone standing on it, and fix what's actually wrong. Many of these letters start with a photo taken from the sky. Sometimes the roof needs a repair, not a new roof. Sometimes the photo got it wrong.

You're not the only one getting these letters

In September 2026, Texas started publishing why insurance companies drop homeowners, ZIP code by ZIP code. We went through the state's data for the 14 counties we serve.

From April through July 2026, insurance companies listed roof condition as a reason to cancel, not renew, or turn down coverage on 1,751 homes in those counties. That's about 440 a month. In 740 of them, about 4 in 10, the company said it used aerial photos.

Why so many roof letters now

  • Photos from the sky. Texas insurance companies use photos taken from airplanes and satellites, and some use computer programs to read them. Homeowner groups say these photos can lead to wrong calls that are hard to fight.
  • Condition, not age. In September 2026, the Texas Department of Insurance made clear that a company can't turn you down or drop you just because your roof is old. It can still act on the roof's condition, so letters now point to what the roof looks like.
  • They have to tell you why. A 2025 Texas law requires insurance companies to tell you in writing why they declined, canceled, or didn't renew your home policy.

What kind of letter did you get?

  • Non-renewal. The company won't renew your policy when this term ends.
  • Cancellation. The policy ends before the term is up.
  • Turned down. The company won't write a new policy for you, often when you're buying a house or switching companies.
  • Fix it by a date. The company wants a repair or replacement, or proof the roof is sound, before a deadline.
  • Less roof coverage. The company is switching your roof to actual cash value, which pays less on an older roof, or leaving the roof out of coverage.

Whatever kind it is, find the date on it and write it down.

What to do, step by step

  1. Write down your deadline

    Everything else works backward from that date.

  2. Ask what they found, in writing

    Ask exactly what condition problem they found. If they used a photo, ask for a copy and the date it was taken. Some companies have refused to show homeowners the photos, so ask anyway and keep a copy of what you sent.

  3. Get the roof checked by someone standing on it

    You want dated photos of every slope and a written report. Up close, a roofer can tell real damage from stains, shadows, and old photos.

  4. Fix what's actually wrong

    Plenty of roof problems are repairs, not a new roof. If it's a repair, we'll tell you. If the roof is worn out, we'll tell you that too.

  5. Send the proof to your agent

    Send the report, the photos, and any receipts. Ask whether the company will keep your policy or put your roof coverage back. Your insurance company makes that call.

  6. Shop around if you need to

    Another company may look at your roof differently. Independent agents can check several companies at once.

  7. Still stuck? Call the state

    The Texas Department of Insurance help line is 800-252-3439. If you think your company dropped you unfairly, you can file a complaint at tdi.texas.gov.
