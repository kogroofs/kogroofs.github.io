How we figure it

  1. The size of your roof. Your living area divided by the stories, plus the garage, plus 10 percent for the overhangs, times a slope factor: 1.07 for a low roof, 1.16 for medium, and 1.35 for steep. It's the same math as our roof cost calculator.
  2. The price. The squares of roof, times our price per square: $995–$1,325 for standing seam, $645–$860 for exposed-fastener metal panels, and $500–$750 for architectural shingles. A square is 100 square feet of roof.
  3. The difference. We take the middle of each price range, so the gap between metal and shingles isn't stretched by the high end of one range and the low end of another.
  4. The credit. Your premium times the credit your agent quotes gives you a yearly savings. The gap divided by that savings is how many years the credit takes to cover it, if your premium and credit stay the same.

Standing seam or metal panels?

Standing seam panels lock together at raised seams, with the fasteners hidden under clips. There are no exposed screws to wear out, and the roof looks clean. It's the premium choice for homes.

Exposed-fastener panels are screwed straight through to the framing or deck. They cost less and go on faster. The trade-off is upkeep: the rubber washers under the screws wear out over the years, and the screws need to be checked. Read more in is a metal roof worth it?

What moves the price of a metal roof

  • Gauge. A lower number is thicker steel. 24 gauge is heavier than 26 or 29, and thicker panels resist dents better.
  • Paint finish. PVDF finishes, often sold as Kynar 500, hold their color longer than lower-cost polyester finishes like SMP.
  • Trim and flashing. Valleys, walls, chimneys, and skylights all need flashing, and that's where most metal roof problems start.
  • What comes off. Extra layers of shingles, or wood shakes that need a whole new deck.
  • The deck. Rotten decking gets replaced at $95 a sheet.
  • The shape of the roof. Steep pitches, two stories, and lots of hips and valleys all add to the price.

Metal roofs and your insurance

A metal roof can lower your premium, but not every company gives a credit, and the amount varies. Texas insurers can give a credit for roofing tested for impact and rated Class 1 through Class 4, and Class 4 gets the most. Before you buy, ask your agent three things:

  • How much credit will you give? That's the number for the calculator above.
  • Does the credit come with a cosmetic damage exclusion? Hail can dent metal without making it leak. With that exclusion, your policy won't pay to fix dents that only change how the roof looks.
  • What proof do you need? When we install a roof rated for impact, we fill out and sign the state's Impact-Resistant Roofing Installation Form (PC068). You keep the original and send a copy to your insurance company.

Insurance companies can change terms when your policy renews, so check your roof coverage every year.

Common questions

How much does a metal roof cost in Dallas-Fort Worth?

At our posted prices, exposed-fastener metal panels run $645 to $860 a square, and standing seam runs $995 to $1,325 a square. A square is 100 square feet of roof. For a one-story, 2,000-square-foot home with a 2-car garage and a medium roof, that's roughly $20,600 to $27,500 for panels and $31,800 to $42,400 for standing seam, before extras.

Is a metal roof worth the extra cost?

Often, if you'll keep the home a long time. A well-installed metal roof can last 40 years or more, it doesn't burn, and if it's rated for impact it can earn a premium credit. Weigh that against the higher price up front, and ask your agent how your policy handles cosmetic hail damage on metal.

Can a metal roof go over my old shingles?

Some metal systems can go over one layer of shingles where the code allows, which saves the cost of the tear-off. A tear-off costs more, but it lets us see and fix the deck before it's covered for decades. If the roof has soft spots, it comes off. The prices in this calculator include the tear-off.

Is this a quote?

No. It's a ballpark from our posted prices. Odd-shaped roofs, big porches, and very steep pitches throw it off, so your written price comes after we measure the roof itself.
