Do roofers need a license in Texas?

Updated October 2026 · For Texas homeowners

The short answer

No. Texas doesn't require a state license to work as a roofer, and the state agency that licenses trades like electricians and air conditioning contractors doesn't cover roofing. That means anyone can print business cards after a hailstorm. So check the things that do tell you something: insurance, a local address, permits, reviews, and a written contract and warranty.

What "no state license" means

There's no state roofing exam, no state roofing license, and no state agency reviewing roofers in Texas. Some roofers carry voluntary credentials from trade groups or shingle makers. Those can be a good sign, but they're optional, and they aren't a state license.

Your city may have its own rules

  • Some cities require registration and a permit for every re-roof. Denton, for example, requires a roof permit for all residential re-roofs, issued before the tear-off starts, and requires the roofer to be registered with the city.
  • Some don't for a simple shingle job. In Fort Worth, replacing shingles on a one- or two-family home doesn't need a permit unless decking is replaced.
  • Outside city limits, the rules can be different. Ask your roofer what your address needs, and who pulls the permit.

Six things to check instead of a license

  1. Insurance

    Ask for a certificate of insurance sent straight from the roofer's insurance agent, showing general liability. Texas doesn't require most private employers to carry workers' compensation, so ask whether the crews on your roof are covered if someone gets hurt.

  2. A local address and phone number

    Someone you can find next year, when a warranty matters.

  3. Permits and city registration

    Where your city requires them, the roofer should be registered and pull the permit before work starts.

  4. Reviews from people near you

    Read reviews on more than one site, and check the Better Business Bureau for complaints.

  5. A written contract and a written warranty

    Labor and materials, in writing. Here's what a roofing contract should include.

  6. No deductible games

    A roofer who offers to waive or cover your deductible is breaking Texas law. Walk away.
