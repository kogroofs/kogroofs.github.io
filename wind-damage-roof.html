How strong does wind have to be to damage a roof?

Updated October 2026 · For Texas homeowners

The short answer

Less than most people think. The National Weather Service calls a thunderstorm severe once winds reach 58 mph, and gusts like that can lift, crease, and tear off older shingles. Shingles are tested and rated for wind, but age, how they were nailed, and the edges of the roof matter as much as the number on the wrapper.

What shingle wind ratings mean

Asphalt shingles are rated by one of two tests. The ratings tell you how much wind the shingle held up to in the lab:

  • ASTM D3161: Class A is rated for 60 mph, Class D for 90 mph, and Class F for 110 mph.
  • ASTM D7158: Class D is rated for 115 mph, Class G for 150 mph, and Class H for 190 mph.

You'll find the rating on the shingle wrapper or the maker's spec sheet. A rating only holds up when the shingle was installed to the maker's instructions.

Why roofs fail below their rating

  • Age. Years of Texas sun dry shingles out, and the sealant strips that hold each course down get weaker.
  • Nailing. Nails set too high, too few, or driven crooked let wind get under a shingle.
  • Edges, corners, and ridges. Wind pulls hardest at the edges of a roof, which is why damage usually starts there.
  • Trees and debris. Limbs over the roof and flying debris do damage at any wind speed.

Signs of wind damage

  • Shingles or pieces of shingle in the yard
  • Missing shingles, or bare spots where you can see the dark underlayment
  • Edges that are lifted or flapping
  • Creased shingles, with a line across them where the wind folded them back. These are hard to see from the ground, but they can leak.
  • Ridge cap pieces missing, or bent flashing

Look from the ground. Binoculars help. Please don't climb on the roof after a storm.

After a windstorm

  1. Walk around the house

    Look at each side of the roof, the gutters, and the yard. Take dated photos.

  3. Get it inspected

    Creased shingles and lifted seals are easy to miss from the ground.

Getting a roof ready for the next storm

When a roof gets replaced, ask what wind rating the new shingle carries, and make sure the starter strips, drip edge, and ridge cap go on to the maker's instructions. On a KOG roof, they do. Got a few shingles missing now? That's often a repair, not a new roof.
