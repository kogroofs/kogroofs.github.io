What will my roof claim cost me?

Your share of a Texas hail or wind claim, in plain words, with a calculator. Updated October 2026.

The short answer

Insurance pays for a storm-damaged roof, but not all of it. Your share starts with your deductible, and in Texas that's now usually a percentage of what your home is insured for, not a flat $1,000. If your policy pays a depreciated value for your roof, your share also includes what the roof lost to age. Put in your numbers below and you'll see your share before anyone tears off a shingle.

How a Texas roof claim pays, step by step

  1. The storm, then the inspection

    We get on the roof, photograph the damage, and give you a written report. It's free.

  2. You file the claim

    The claim is between you and your insurance company. Call the number on your policy or use their app.

  3. The adjuster writes an estimate

    It lists what they'll pay for, the price, any depreciation, and your deductible. We can meet the adjuster on the roof and point out what we found.

  4. The first check

    On a replacement-cost policy, the first check is usually the roof's depreciated value minus your deductible. The insurance company holds back the depreciation until the work is done. If you have a mortgage, the check may also carry your mortgage company's name, and they'll need to sign it too.

  5. The roof gets done

    You pay your deductible to the roofer, the way your contract says.

  6. The second check

    After the roof is finished, the final invoice goes to your insurance company and they release the depreciation they held back. On a depreciated-value policy, there's usually no second check for the roof. That gap is yours to pay.

Every policy is a little different. Your policy and your adjuster's estimate are the final word.

What changed in Texas

  • Deductibles became percentages. A 2% wind and hail deductible is now the standard across most of Texas, especially North Texas. On a home insured for $350,000, that's $7,000 before insurance pays anything. Texas has the highest wind and hail deductibles in the country as a share of a home's insured value, about 2.2%, or $7,761, on average.
  • Older roofs get paid less. Many insurance companies now pay older shingle roofs at a depreciated value, or on a payment schedule by the roof's age, even when the rest of the house is covered at full replacement cost. Some won't insure a roof past about 15 years old.
  • Some hail damage isn't covered at all. A cosmetic damage exclusion leaves out dents that don't hurt how the roof works. Read what a cosmetic damage exclusion is.
  • Some people stop filing. With deductibles this high, some homeowners skip the claim and live with a damaged roof.

Where to find your numbers

Your declarations page. It's the first page or two of your policy. It shows what your home is insured for, usually as Coverage A or Dwelling, and your deductibles. Look for a separate line for wind and hail.

Your endorsements. These are the add-on pages that change your policy. Look for words like "roof surfacing," "payment schedule," "actual cash value," and "cosmetic damage."

Still not sure? Ask your agent these two questions:

  1. What is my wind and hail deductible, in dollars?
  2. Is my roof paid at replacement cost or depreciated value?

Common questions

Is my deductible a percentage of the damage?

No. A percentage deductible is figured from what your home is insured for, not from the size of the claim. A 2% deductible on a home insured for $300,000 is $6,000, whether the roof job costs $9,000 or $30,000.

What if the roof job costs less than my deductible?

Then insurance pays nothing for it, and the job is yours to pay.

When do I get the depreciation that was held back?

On a replacement-cost policy, usually after the work is done and your insurance company gets the final invoice. Many policies set a time limit to finish the work, so check yours.

How did you estimate depreciation?

We take off 3% for each year of the roof's age, the same rate as the Texas Department of Insurance's own example, so a 10-year-old roof comes out 30% depreciated. Insurance companies use their own tables, so your adjuster's number is the one that counts. If you have it, put it in the calculator.

Can KOG lower my share?

Not by touching your deductible. Texas law doesn't allow it. What we can do is make sure the adjuster sees all the storm damage, tell you straight when a repair makes more sense than a claim, and let you pay the deductible over time with no interest.
