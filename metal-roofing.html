Metal roofing in Dallas-Fort Worth

New metal roofs and metal roof repair for homes, shops, and commercial buildings

The short answer

We install standing seam and exposed-fastener metal roofs, and we repair metal roofs other companies put on. Most metal roof leaks come from the screws, the trim, and the flashing, not the panels, so that's where we look first.

Where metal makes sense

  • Homes. Standing seam, with hidden fasteners and clean lines, for owners who want a roof they won't think about again for a long time.
  • Shops, barns, and garages. Exposed-fastener panels go on fast over purlins or a solid deck and cost less than standing seam.
  • Metal buildings and warehouses. Panel roofs on pre-engineered buildings, where the roof is part of the building's design.
  • Offices, churches, and storefronts. Standing seam on the sloped sections, often next to a flat roof on the rest of the building. We do both. See commercial roofing.

How a new metal roof goes

  1. Measure and choose. We measure the roof and go over panel type, gauge, and color with you. Metal panels are ordered cut to length for your roof, so the numbers have to be right the first time.
  2. Get approvals. If you're in an HOA or a historic district, switching to metal or changing color may need approval first. We give you the product name, color, and spec sheet for the request.
  3. Prep the deck. Tear-off where it's needed, decking repairs, and new underlayment.
  4. Trim and flashing. Eave trim, rake trim, valleys, and wall flashing go on in the right order so each piece sheds water over the one below it.
  5. Panels. Installed square and fastened to the manufacturer's pattern.
  6. Penetrations and ridge. Pipe flashings sized to each pipe, then the ridge cap with closures that keep out water, wind-driven rain, and wasps.
  7. Final walk. We check every screw line and every piece of trim, clean up the metal shavings, and give you photos of the finished roof.

Metal roof repair

Older exposed-fastener roofs on shops and warehouses often start leaking at the screws long before the panels wear out. Common problems and fixes:

  • Backed-out screws and dried-out washers. Panels grow and shrink with the heat every day, and that works the screws loose. We replace them with new screws, sized up where the old holes have wallowed out.
  • Leaks at panel laps. Where one panel overlaps the next, failed sealant lets water wick in. We clean and reseal the laps.
  • Trim and flashing. Loose or missing ridge cap, gable trim, and wall flashing, often after high wind.
  • Pipe and vent flashings. The rubber boots around pipes dry out and crack in the sun, just like on a shingle roof.
  • Skylight panels. Clear panels on shops and barns get brittle and crack. We replace them to match the rib profile.
  • Rust. Surface rust at cut edges and screw holes can be treated if it's caught early. Rust that's eaten through the panel means that panel gets replaced.

Common questions

Can you match the panels on my building?

Usually. Panel profiles vary by manufacturer, so we measure the rib spacing and height and find a match. Color is harder: new paint won't match panels that have faded for years.

Does a metal roof attract lightning?

No. A metal roof doesn't make a lightning strike more likely.

Will hail ruin a metal roof?

Large hail can dent metal, but dents usually don't make it leak. Before you buy, ask your insurance agent how your policy handles cosmetic damage to a metal roof.

Do you work on metal roofs you didn't install?

Yes. Most of the metal roof repairs we do are on roofs someone else put on.
