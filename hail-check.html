How this works

NOAA's network of NEXRAD weather radars scans every storm every few minutes. When a storm is making hail, the radar estimates where it's falling and how big the stones are, and NOAA keeps those records. Every night we pull the records for the Dallas-Fort Worth area and the counties around it, so you can look up any address.

Radar sees the storm, not your roof. Sizes are estimates, and hail on the ground can be bigger or smaller. A storm can also miss one street and hit the next. Only an inspection can tell you whether your roof was damaged.

Common questions

Does hail always damage a roof?

No. It depends on the size of the hail, the wind behind it, and the roof's age and type. Older shingles can be damaged by smaller hail than a new roof can take. Hail damage often doesn't leak right away, which is why it's worth having a roof checked after a big storm.

Is this the weather data my insurance company uses?

Insurance companies use their own weather reports, and they decide a claim based on what's actually on your roof. Use this hail check as a starting point, not as proof of damage.

How far back do the records go?

About three years. The results show the exact start date, and the records are updated every night.

Do you save the addresses I check?

No. Your address goes to the U.S. Census Bureau's free address service to get map coordinates, and the hail search runs on your own phone or computer. We only see your address if you send it to us through a form.

My address shows no hail. Could my roof still be damaged?

Yes. Radar can miss small or short bursts of hail, and wind, age, and poor installation damage roofs too. If you see shingles in the yard, granules in the gutters, or a stain on a ceiling, have it checked.
