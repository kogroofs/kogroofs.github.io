Storm damage inspections and emergency tarping in Dallas-Fort Worth

Hail and wind damage on homes and commercial buildings

The short answer

After hail or high wind, we get on the roof, document what the storm did slope by slope, and give you a written report with photos. You decide what to do with it. If it's a claim, you file it with your insurance company, and we can meet your adjuster on the roof to point out the damage we found.

24-hour emergency tarping

If a storm opened up your roof and water is coming in, call us any time, day or night. We cover the damage with tarps or synthetic underlayment to keep the water out until the roof can be repaired, and we photograph the damage before we cover it so your insurance company can see what the storm did.

Keep the receipt. Most policies expect you to take reasonable steps to prevent more damage, and the cost of emergency tarping is often part of the claim. Ask your insurance company how yours handles it.

What we check

Shingle roofs

  • Hail hits on every slope: spots where granules were knocked off and the mat underneath is bruised or cracked
  • Wind damage: missing shingles, shingles creased where they lifted, and torn or loose ridge caps
  • Pipe boots, vents, and flashing that took hits or were pulled loose

Flat roofs

  • Punctures, cracks, and fractures in the membrane, which can be hard to see without getting close
  • Dents in metal edge, coping, and gutters
  • Rooftop units, skylights, drains, and anything the wind moved

Metal roofs

  • Dents in panels, seams, and trim, and whether any of them opened the roof to water
  • Bent or lifted trim and ridge cap

Everything around the roof

  • Dents in gutters, downspouts, vents, and AC fins. Hail marks on soft metal help show how big the stones were and which way they came from.
  • The attic and ceilings, for water that already got in

What's in your report

  • Photos of each slope and each kind of damage, close up and wide, so it's clear where every photo was taken
  • Roof type, layers, and approximate age
  • What we found, slope by slope, and whether it looks like storm damage or normal wear
  • Our honest opinion: repair, replace, or leave it alone
  • A written estimate if work is needed

Not every storm damages a roof. If yours came through fine, the report says so, and it's still yours to keep for your records.

Your claim, your call

The claim is between you and your insurance company. We don't negotiate it and we don't act as your adjuster. What we do:

  • Give you the report and photos to send to your insurance company
  • Meet your adjuster on the roof to point out the damage we found
  • Do the work your insurer approves, at the price in your contract

You pay your deductible. A roofer who offers to cover it, waive it, or hide it in a "rebate" is breaking Texas law. For the first 48 hours after a storm and how to spot a storm chaser, read After a hail or wind storm.

Commercial buildings after a storm

For property managers, a storm can mean several roofs to check at once. We inspect each building, put the findings in a written report with photos for each property, and tell you which ones need attention first. Hail damage on a flat roof often doesn't leak right away, so it's worth having it checked before it turns into a ceiling-tile problem months later. See commercial roofing.

Common questions

How big does hail have to be to damage a roof?

It depends on the roof's age and type. The National Weather Service calls hail severe at one inch, about the size of a quarter. Older shingles can be damaged by smaller hail than a new roof can take.

How soon should I get my roof checked?

Soon. Most policies set a time limit for reporting damage, and hail damage that isn't leaking today can start leaking later. Check your policy for its deadline.

Water is coming in right now. What do I do?

Call us, day or night. We'll tell you how to protect your belongings, and we'll get out there and tarp the roof to stop the water. Keep the receipts for anything you spend to stop more damage.

Do you inspect roofs another company installed?

Yes. Who put the roof on doesn't change what the storm did to it.

Did a storm come through?

Call for an inspection. You'll get photos and a written report, and an honest answer about whether you have damage.
