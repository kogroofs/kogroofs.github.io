Roof replacement across Dallas-Fort Worth

Shingle and metal roofs, torn off down to the deck

The short answer

We tear the old roof off down to the deck, fix any bad wood, and build the new roof up from there with new underlayment, flashing, vents, and pipe boots. You get the scope and price in writing before we start, and photos of the parts you'll never see once the shingles are on.

What's in a KOG roof replacement

  1. Tear-off to the deck. All the old layers come off, so nothing hides under the new roof.
  2. Decking check. We walk the bare deck and replace soft, rotted, or delaminated boards, and show you photos of what we replaced.
  3. Underlayment. New underlayment over the whole deck, installed to the shingle maker's instructions.
  4. Drip edge and flashing. New drip edge at the eaves and rakes, and new flashing at walls, chimneys, and valleys. Old flashing doesn't get reused.
  5. Pipe boots and vents. New boots on every plumbing pipe and new roof vents, so the cheapest parts of the roof aren't the first to fail.
  6. Ventilation. We check the intake at the eaves and the exhaust at the ridge. A Texas attic that can't breathe cooks the shingles from underneath.
  7. Shingles, starter, and ridge cap. Installed to the manufacturer's nailing pattern.
  8. Cleanup. Daily cleanup and a magnetic sweep of the yard and driveway for nails.
  9. Final photos and walkthrough. You see the finished roof, top to bottom.

Choosing shingles

  • Architectural shingles. The standard for most homes today: thicker than old three-tab shingles, with a dimensional look.
  • Impact-resistant shingles. Rated to take harder hail (Class 4 under UL 2218). In hail country they're worth a look, and some insurers give a discount for them. Ask your agent before you choose.
  • Color. Lighter colors soak up less heat. If you're in an HOA, check what colors are allowed before ordering.

When insurance is paying

If hail or wind damaged your roof, the replacement may be covered by your homeowner's policy. Here's how it usually goes:

  1. Inspection. We inspect and photograph the damage and tell you honestly whether it looks like a claim.
  2. You file the claim with your insurance company. The claim is between you and your insurer.
  3. Adjuster visit. Your insurer sends an adjuster. We can be on the roof with them to point out what we found.
  4. Scope and payment. Your insurer decides what's covered. Many policies pay part up front and the rest once the work is done.
  5. Your deductible. You pay it. Any roofer who offers to cover or waive it is breaking Texas law.

Common questions

How long does a roof replacement take?

Most homes take one to two days, weather permitting. Bigger or steeper roofs, or a lot of rotted decking, can take longer.

Do I need to be home?

Not the whole time. We'll walk the job with you at the start and the finish. Move cars out of the driveway and anything fragile off the walls inside. Hammering shakes things.

What's your warranty?

Five years on labor for roofs 4/12 and steeper, one year on low-slope roofs, plus the manufacturer's material warranty.

Do I need a permit?

It depends on your city and what the job involves. Some cities don't require one for a straight shingle replacement but do once decking is replaced. We check for your address and handle any permit before work starts.
