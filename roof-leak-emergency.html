Roof leaking in the rain? What to do right now

Updated October 2026

The short answer

Keep everyone safe, catch the water, and stay off the roof. If water is near lights or outlets, shut off that breaker. Take photos. Then call (817) 550-2339, day or night. We'll come tarp the roof so no more water gets in, and fix the leak once it's dry.

Do this first

  1. Keep water away from electricity

    If water is dripping near a light fixture, ceiling fan, outlet, or your electrical panel, turn off the breaker for that part of the house, but only if you can reach the panel without standing in water. If a ceiling is sagging hard or cracking, get everyone out of that room.

  2. Catch the water and move your things

    Buckets, pots, and towels under the drips. Move furniture, electronics, and rugs out of the way, and cover what you can't move with plastic.

  3. Drain a bulging ceiling

    If the drywall is holding a bubble of water, put a bucket under the lowest point, stand to the side, and poke a small hole with a screwdriver. Letting it drain slowly beats having the whole patch come down at once.

  4. Find where it's getting in, if it's safe

    With a flashlight, look in the attic for wet decking, drips, or soaked insulation. Step only on the framing, never between it. Water often runs along a rafter before it drips, so the hole in the roof can be several feet from the spot on your ceiling.

  5. Take photos and video

    Get the ceiling, the attic, anything damaged, and the roof from the ground. Dated photos help your insurance company, and they help us find the leak faster.

  6. Stay off the roof

    A wet roof is slick, wind can rip a tarp right out of your hands, and lightning finds the highest point. Tarping a roof in the rain at night is a job for a crew with the right gear.

  7. Make the calls

    Call us at (817) 550-2339, any hour. If a storm caused the leak, call your insurance company too and get a claim number.

What happens when you call KOG

We answer emergency calls 24/7. When we get there, we photograph the damage first, so there's a record of what happened. Then we cover the problem area with a heavy tarp or synthetic underlayment, fastened so the wind can't get under it, to keep the water out until the roof is dry enough to fix right.

Emergency tarping runs about $150 to $3,300, depending on how much of the roof needs covering, and night and weekend calls can cost more. You'll know the price before we start. Keep the receipt. If it's an insurance claim, most policies expect you to take reasonable steps to prevent more damage, and the tarp is often part of the claim.

When the weather clears, we come back, find the real source, and give you a written report with photos and a price to fix it. Most leaks are a repair, not a new roof.

Where roof leaks usually start

On Dallas-Fort Worth roofs, these are the spots we find most:

  • Pipe boots. The rubber collar around a plumbing vent cracks in the Texas sun. It's one of the most common leaks we fix, and one of the cheapest.
  • Flashing. Where the roof meets a chimney, a wall, or a skylight, loose or rusted flashing lets water slip in behind it.
  • Missing or lifted shingles after high wind.
  • Hail damage. Cracked or bruised shingles often don't leak until months after the storm.
  • Valleys, where two slopes meet and the most water runs.
  • Nail pops, where a nail backs out and lifts the shingle above it.
  • Flat roofs: open seams, punctures, and clogged drains that let water pond.
  • Not the roof at all. Clogged gutters can overflow behind the fascia and look just like a roof leak.

Most repairs run about $150 to $1,500, and a pipe boot about $75 to $250. See our price ranges and roof repair.

After the rain stops

  • Dry it out fast. Run fans and a dehumidifier and pull up wet rugs. Mold can start growing within 24 to 48 hours.
  • Check the attic insulation. Soaked insulation holds water against the ceiling and stops insulating. It may need to come out.
  • Watch the stain. A ceiling stain that keeps growing means water is still getting in. More on what a ceiling stain can mean.
  • Get the inside fixed too. When a leak damages ceilings, walls, or floors, KOG repairs those after the roof is fixed, so you deal with one company from the roof to the inside.

Will insurance pay for a roof leak?

It depends on what caused it. If a storm damaged the roof, the roof and the water damage it caused are often covered, minus your deductible. If the leak came from age or wear, most policies won't pay to fix the roof itself, though some cover sudden water damage inside the house. Ask your agent.
