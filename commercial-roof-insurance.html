Commercial roofing

Your insurance company wants proof your roof is cared for. We give you the proof.

For building owners, property managers, and facility managers · Dallas-Fort Worth

The short answer

Insurance companies are judging commercial roofs more closely than ever. Buildings that can show a well-kept roof are in the best spot at renewal. We inspect the roof, give you a dated photo report you can hand to your insurance agent, fix the roof items on your insurance company's inspection list, and send you proof the work is done.

What's changing for commercial buildings

  • Prices are easing, but not for everyone. Commercial property insurance rates have come down in 2026, but brokers say most of that relief is going to well-managed buildings. Paperwork and preparation matter more than they used to.
  • Roofs get paid less. Many commercial policies carry a roof surfacing endorsement. It can pay the roof at actual cash value, meaning after wear and age are taken off, and leave out hail or wind damage that's only cosmetic.
  • Big deductibles, building by building. Wind and hail deductibles are often a percentage of the building's insured value, and some policies apply one to each building. At 2%, a $5 million building carries $100,000 per building for each storm. A lot of roof damage now comes out of the owner's pocket.
  • Inspection lists with deadlines. After an insurance company inspects a building, it can send recommendations. Some are advisory. Some are mandatory.
  • Roofs graded from the sky. Insurance companies can buy reports that score a commercial roof's condition and age from aerial photos, and flag buildings that look worse at renewal time.
  • Tougher hail standards. FM Global, a major commercial insurer, expanded its “Very Severe Hail” zones in 2025, and Texas is one of the states where those zones grew.

What we do

  1. A roof report for your insurance file

    We walk the whole roof and photograph every section, plus the seams, flashing, drains, and rooftop units. You get a dated written report: what kind of roof it is, its condition, what needs attention now, what can wait, and about how many years it has left. It's written so your insurance agent can hand it straight to the insurance company.

  2. Your insurance inspection list, done

    Got a list of roof items to fix by a deadline? Send it to us. We price each item in writing, do the work, and send you before-and-after photos with a letter listing what was done and when. You forward it to your agent.

  3. Spring and fall checks

    A maintenance plan keeps drains clear and small problems small, with dated photos from every visit. If a storm hits, you have proof of what the roof looked like before it.

  4. A budget you can plan around

    What the roof needs now, in the next 12 months, and in 3 to 5 years, with prices, so roof money goes in the budget instead of hitting as a surprise.

  5. When it's time for a new roof

    We look up your building's hail zone and walk you through tougher, hail-resistant roof options before you pick one.

What you get from it

  • Proof at renewal. A dated report shows your agent and your insurance company the real condition of the roof, not just what a photo from the sky suggests.
  • Deadlines covered. We price your list fast, work to your deadline, and document every item.
  • Fewer surprises. With big deductibles, the cheapest roof damage is the damage that never happens. Maintenance and small repairs cost far less than leaks into tenant space.
  • Before-and-after records. If a storm hits, dated photos from before it help separate new damage from old.
