Hail and storm damage

How big does hail have to be to damage a roof?

It depends on the roof's age and type. The National Weather Service calls hail severe at 1 inch, about the size of a quarter, and that's where shingle damage gets common. Older, sun-baked shingles can be damaged by smaller hail than a new roof can take, and wind behind the hail makes it worse.

How can I tell if hail damaged my roof without climbing on it?

Look at the soft metal around your house: dents in gutters, downspouts, vents, and the fins on your AC unit. Check for shingle granules piled at the bottom of downspouts, pieces of shingle in the yard, and new stains on ceilings. Please stay off the roof. We'll get up there and photograph what we find. More in how to tell if your roof has hail damage.

How do I know if hail hit my house?

Check your address on our free hail check. It shows the days NOAA weather radar detected hail near your home and how big it was, going back about three years. Radar can't see your roof, so it tells you where to look, not whether you have damage.

Does hail damage always leak right away?

No. Hail often bruises shingles and knocks off granules without causing a leak. The damage shows up later, as shingles crack and wear out faster. That's why it's worth an inspection after a big storm even if nothing is dripping.

Water is coming in right now. What should I do?

Move what you can out of the way, catch the water, and stay off the roof. Then call us at (817) 550-2339, day or night. We'll come tarp the roof to stop the water, and we photograph the damage first so your insurance company can see what the storm did. Here's what to do while you wait.

How strong does wind have to be to damage shingles?

Less than most people think. The National Weather Service calls a storm severe at 58 mph winds, and gusts like that can lift and crease older shingles, even ones rated higher on the wrapper. What shingle wind ratings mean, and the signs of wind damage.

Insurance

Want your own number? Our roof claim calculator shows your share of a hail claim from your deductible, your roof's age, and how your policy pays.

Will my homeowners insurance pay for a new roof after hail?

It depends on your policy and on what the storm actually did to your roof. Hail is usually a covered peril, but your deductible, how your policy pays for roofs, and any exclusions decide what you'd get. Read your declarations page and ask your agent. We'll give you a written report and photos of what we find to send to your insurance company. Read the full guide: Will insurance pay for a new roof after hail?

What is a wind and hail deductible?

Many Texas policies have a separate deductible for wind and hail that's a percentage of your home's insured value instead of a flat dollar amount. A 2% deductible is now common in North Texas. On a home insured for $300,000, a 2% deductible is $6,000, so you'd pay the first $6,000 of a hail claim.

Can I make payments on my deductible?

Yes. Our deductible payment plan is at least 20% down ($250 minimum), then up to 4 monthly payments, with no interest, no fees, and no credit check. You still pay the full deductible. Texas law requires it, and we never waive it.

What's the difference between ACV and RCV roof coverage?

Replacement cost value (RCV) coverage pays what it costs to replace the roof, minus your deductible. Actual cash value (ACV) coverage pays what the roof was worth before the storm, after depreciation for its age, minus your deductible. On an older roof, ACV can pay much less. Your declarations page, or an endorsement on your policy, tells you which one you have.

What is a cosmetic damage exclusion?

It's a part of some policies that excludes hail damage that only changes how the roof looks, like dents in metal panels, without hurting how the roof works. Texas has allowed it since the late 1990s, and it's common on metal and impact-resistant roofs. Search your policy for the words "cosmetic damage."

Can a roofer pay or waive my deductible?

No. It's illegal in Texas for a contractor to offer to waive, rebate, or absorb your deductible. If a roofer offers, walk away. Texas also requires a contract of $1,000 or more that's paid by an insurance settlement to include a notice that you must pay your deductible.

Will KOG handle my insurance claim for me?

The claim is between you and your insurance company. Texas doesn't allow a contractor doing the work to act as your public adjuster or negotiate your claim. What we do: give you a written report and photos to send to your insurer, meet your adjuster on the roof to point out the damage we found, and do the work your insurer approves at the price in your contract.

Do impact-resistant (Class 4) shingles lower my insurance?

Often. Roofing rated Class 4 under the UL 2218 impact test earns the biggest discount with many insurers, and each insurer sets its own amount. Some carriers pair the discount with a cosmetic damage exclusion, so ask your agent about both before you decide.

How long do I have to file a hail claim in Texas?

Check your policy. Many Texas policies give you one year from the storm to report the damage, and a separate deadline, usually about two years, limits how long you have to take a dispute to court. Every deadline on a hail claim.

Can my insurance drop me because my roof is old?

Not for age alone. In 2026, the Texas Department of Insurance said insurers can't deny or refuse to renew a home policy just because the roof is old. They can still look at its condition. What to do if you get a letter about your roof.

Will filing a roof claim raise my rates?

Not a hail or wind claim by itself. Texas says your company can't raise your premium or refuse to renew because of claims for weather damage. Your rate can still rise for other reasons. What Texas law says about claims and rates.

Do I have to use my insurance company's roofer?

No. In Texas, you're entitled to have your home repaired by the person of your choice. Your insurance company can suggest roofers, but you don't have to use them. What that means for your claim and your check.

Only part of my roof is damaged. Will insurance replace all of it?

Not necessarily. Your company might not replace the whole roof even if new shingles won't match perfectly, and Texas has no law that requires matching. Your policy's wording decides it. When a whole roof is more likely, and what to check.

Should I sign anything before the adjuster comes?

You don't have to sign anything to get your roof inspected or file a claim. If a roofer hands you papers first, read them for what happens if the claim is denied, and never give away rights to your claim. What to read for before you sign.

Costs

How much does a roof repair cost in Dallas-Fort Worth?

Most repairs fall between about $150 and $1,500. Replacing a pipe boot runs about $75 to $250, replacing missing or damaged shingles $150 to $700, flashing repair $200 to $600, and a leak repair $350 to $1,500, depending on what failed and how hard it is to reach. You get a written price before we start. See our price ranges.

How much does a new roof cost in Dallas-Fort Worth?

Architectural shingles run about $500 to $750 per square, installed, with the old roof torn off. A square is 100 square feet of roof. A roof with 2,000 square feet of roof area, or 20 squares, comes to roughly $10,000 to $15,000 before extras like decking repairs. Roof area is usually bigger than a home's floor area because of the slope and overhangs. See our price ranges.

Only for your materials. On a roof you pay for yourself, you pay for your materials when you sign, and you see them delivered to your driveway before work starts. You pay the rest the day your roof is done and you've walked it with us. Read the KOG Straight Deal.

Why do roof quotes vary so much?

Two quotes that are far apart usually aren't for the same roof. One includes new flashing, pipe boots, decking, and ventilation work, and the other reuses them or leaves them out. Compare them line by line. How to compare roof quotes.

Repair or replace

Should I repair or replace my roof?

Most leaks start in one place, like a cracked pipe boot or loose flashing, and a repair fixes them for a fraction of the cost of a new roof. A replacement makes sense when damage is spread across the roof, the shingles are worn out, or repairs would keep adding up. Our guide walks through the signs: Roof repair or replacement? How long does a shingle roof last in North Texas?

Basic three-tab shingles often last 15 to 20 years and architectural shingles 25 to 30, less under hard sun, heat, and hail. Age alone doesn't mean you need a new roof. Condition does.

How long does a roof replacement take?

Most homes take one to two days, weather permitting. Commercial roofs depend on the size and type of roof.

Do I need a permit to replace my roof?

It depends on the city and the job. In Fort Worth, replacing shingles on a one- or two-family home doesn't need a permit unless decking is replaced. Other cities have their own rules, and the rules can be different inside and outside city limits. We check what your address needs and get any permit before work starts.

Can you put new shingles over the old ones?

We tear off all the old layers so nothing hides under the new roof, and so we can check the decking and fix any that's rotten before the new roof goes on.

Do you repair roofs another company installed?

Yes. Most of the leaks we fix are on roofs someone else put on.

Is a metal roof worth it with Texas hail?

Often, if you plan to keep the building a long time. A well-installed metal roof can last 40 years or more, but large hail can dent it. Usually the dents are cosmetic and the roof still keeps water out. Read Is a metal roof worth it?

What's the best shingle for Texas heat and hail?

For most North Texas homes, a Class 4 impact-resistant architectural shingle over an attic that breathes. Not every Class 4 shingle performs the same, so ask which product you're getting. Choosing a shingle for Texas.

Is a small water stain on my ceiling a big deal?

It can be. The wet area above is usually bigger than the stain, and it isn't always the roof. Mark the edge in pencil, and check it after the next rain. If it grows, water is still getting in. Roof, AC line, or plumbing? How to tell.

How often should I have my roof inspected?

Once a year, and after hail an inch or bigger or severe winds. Roofing contractors' national association recommends twice a year, and the Texas Department of Insurance suggests every few years at least. What to check yourself, and when to call.

Commercial roofs

TPO, EPDM, or torch-down: which flat roof is best?

All three are proven low-slope roofs. TPO is a white, heat-welded membrane that reflects the sun. EPDM is a rubber membrane with a long track record. Torch-down modified bitumen is a layered asphalt system that stands up well to foot traffic. The right choice depends on your building, its drainage, how much traffic the roof sees, and your budget. Hiring a roofer

Do roofers need a license in Texas?

No. Texas doesn't license roofers, so check insurance, a local address, permits, reviews, and a written contract and warranty instead. Some cities have their own registration rules. What to check instead of a license.

How do I pick a roofer I can trust?

Start with people you know and roofers working in your neighborhood, then ask about insurance, crews, decking, permits, and warranty. Walk away from pressure to sign today or an offer to waive your deductible. Ten questions to ask any roofer.

What should a roofing contract include?

Everything you were promised, in writing: the work, the materials, the price, the payment schedule, changes, permits, and warranty. Texas also requires a deductible notice on insurance jobs of $1,000 or more. A Texas roofing contract checklist.
